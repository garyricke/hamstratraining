There are three primary ways to operate this machine, ranging from standard remote use to emergency manual overrides.

### **1\. Radio Remote Control**

The radio remote is the typical method of operation and can be powered in several ways:

### **Control Categories and Color Coding**

* **Enable Buttons**: There is an enable button on both the left and right sides of the remote; either one must be held to perform most tasks.

* **Boom Controls**: The remote features dedicated controls for the lower boom, upper boom, and jib.  
* **Drive and Rotation**: Separate controls manage the left and right tracks for driving , while another function allows for rotating the boom.  
* **Outriggers**: Specific controls manage the deployment and retraction of outriggers. Some users may use the front or rear outrigger pairs individually for loading purposes.  
* **Speed Modes**: The remote allows switching between high, medium, and slow (turtle) speeds.

* **Start/Stop**: One toggle is used to shut the machine off or start it with a quick upward push.

To get the machine started, connected, and properly positioned on a job site, follow these instructions:

### **1\. Starting and Connecting the Radio**

The machine uses a two-key system for operation and power management:

* **Select Control Mode**: Use the first key to choose between the **radio** (ground) controls or the **basket** controls.  
* **Power and Cold Start**: The second key provides power to the machine. For a cold start, turn the key to the next stage to engage the **glow plugs**. Wait for the orange light to turn off before firing up the engine.  
* **Radio Connection**: Turn the key on the radio remote and look for a blinking light. Press the **green button** on the remote to establish a connection with the machine; a fully lit display confirms you are connected.

### **2\. Maintenance and Travel Setup**

* **Fuel and Grease**: To easily access the fuel tank and grease points, the boom must be raised and turned sideways.  
* **Track Width**: Keep the tracks in the **extended (out)** position at all times for stability, unless you are navigating through a narrow gate.  
* **Traveling on Slopes**: If the job site is hilly, keep the outriggers partially extended while traveling. This ensures that if the machine tips, it lands on the outriggers rather than damaging the booms.

### **3\. Outrigger Setup and Leveling**

The machine uses outriggers for stabilization, which can be set in two different widths:

* **Position One**: Keeps the outriggers tighter to the machine for narrow areas, but limits turret rotation and distance.  
* **Position Two (Default)**: Fully extends the outriggers and is the standard operating position. Ensure the locking pins are pushed all the way down.  
* **Auto-Leveling Stages**: Use the outrigger switch on the radio.  
  * **Stage 1-3**: Pressing the switch repeatedly lifts the machine through three stages. **Stage 3** is the maximum height while still maintaining the machine's **auto-level** feature.  
  * **Manual Over-extension**: If you press the switch again, the outriggers will extend fully regardless of whether the machine is level.

### **4\. Machine Components**

The lifting system consists of several connected parts that function together:

* **Basket and Jib**: The basket can be disconnected for tight access. It is attached to the **jib**, which provides several feet of extra reach.  
* **Upper Boom**: This single section provides both lifting and **telescoping** functions.  
* **Lower Boom**: Also known as the lower pantograph, this consists of two sections that operate simultaneously to provide vertical lift.

To operate the machine from the basket once it is leveled and the outriggers are down, follow these procedures:

### **1\. Entering Basket Mode**
